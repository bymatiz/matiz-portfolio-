Situation

Food that stops being a meal
the moment it becomes what we live around it.

An online store of artisan frozen meals: plant-based, wood-fire baked, built on regenerative agriculture principles. From regenerative soil to stone-milled grain to wood-fired kitchen - a food brand where the ingredient's journey is part of the flavour.

The central challenge: the customer can't taste before buying. The first purchase requires trust that doesn't yet exist - no history, no reviews, no physical store.

Brand name withheld pending trademark registration.

The challenge

The smell of bread in the oven
doesn't travel through a screen.

The product's visual identity: dark stone, linen, aged wood. Rustic but refined.

The answer wasn't in the photography - though the photography matters. It was in the message, the strategy, and the sequence of moments between the first click and the first real order.

The audience: people who want to eat well and don't have time - but refuse to sacrifice pleasure, quality, and awareness of what they put on the table. No compromises - ethical, nutritional, sensory.

The approach

A three-moment strategy:
image convinces, story involves, flavour retains.

The acquisition funnel was designed as a sequence of experiences - each removing one more layer of hesitation before the first real order.

Moment 01 · The tasting

Choose 5 flavours. Pay only €3.90 in shipping.

Reimbursed in full on the first real order. The leap of faith disappears. The risk is removed before it becomes a reason not to try.

Moment 02 · The form as experience

Personalised in real time.

"Ana Silva, each detail is requested only to prepare your experience with the care it deserves." Feels like composing a meal, not filling a form.

Moment 03 · The surprise

A double benefit the customer didn't expect.

Automated email with sensory language and a surprise 15% voucher - combined with the shipping reimbursement. The second order becomes natural.

The Tally form: designed as an experience, not a checkout step.

Reflection

"Tasting is easy. Returning is natural.

Some things can't be rushed - and the best ones taste like it."

The challenge wasn't technical. It was about making someone feel the warmth of a wood-fired kitchen through a screen - before they'd tasted a single thing. The strategy worked because it removed risk at every step, and replaced it with anticipation.
